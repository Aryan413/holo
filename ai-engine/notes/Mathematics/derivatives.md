# Derivatives

The derivative of a function measures how fast its value changes. Geometrically it is the slope of the tangent line to the graph at a point.

## Definition

The derivative of f at x is the limit of (f(x + h) - f(x)) / h as h approaches 0, when that limit exists. It is written f'(x) or dy/dx.

## Basic rules

The power rule: the derivative of x^n is n × x^(n-1). The derivative of a constant is 0. The derivative of a sum is the sum of the derivatives, and a constant factor can be taken outside.

The product rule: (u × v)' = u' × v + u × v'.

The chain rule: if y = f(g(x)), then dy/dx = f'(g(x)) × g'(x). It is used whenever one function is nested inside another.

## Example

To differentiate y = (3x + 1)^2, let u = 3x + 1, so y = u^2. Then dy/dx = 2u × 3 = 6(3x + 1).
