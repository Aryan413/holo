# Kirchhoff's laws

Kirchhoff's laws describe how current and voltage behave in circuits that are more complex than a single loop.

## Kirchhoff's current law (KCL)

The total current flowing into a junction equals the total current flowing out of it. This follows from conservation of charge: charge cannot pile up at a junction. Written another way, the algebraic sum of the currents at any node is zero.

## Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL)

The algebraic sum of all the voltage rises and drops around any closed loop is zero. This follows from conservation of energy: a charge that returns to its starting point has the same potential as before.

## Using the laws

To analyse a circuit, label the unknown currents, write one KCL equation for each independent junction and one KVL equation for each independent loop, then solve the equations together. Ohm's law supplies the voltage across each resistor.
